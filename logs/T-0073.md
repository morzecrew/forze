## Self-audit — 2026-10-15

**Scope.** The whole branch against `main`: five commits, 45 files, about 2.6k lines (source,
tests, docs, config, this log). The lifecycle step (§5.4, D-7, D-8 and the second half of
D-11) is not built in this pass and was not audited. The ten passes ran on the code, then a
fresh-eyes reviewer read the same diff; both sets of findings are below.

**Checks run.**

- `ruff check`, `ruff format --check` and `mypy` over `src`; the full unit suite
  (`just test tests/unit` after the review fixes: 17,612 passed, 13 skipped); the whole Redis integration suite
  before the review fixes (201 passed), and the fleet's Redis tests after them (18 passed).
- The conformance manifest (`--collect`), docs floors, docs snippets, a full `zensical build`,
  vulture, the changelog validator (150 problems, the baseline) and the RFC index check
  (46 problems and 39 warnings, the baseline).
- **Sabotage.** Every mutant was killed by a named test:
  - Redis: key expiry taken from the beat's own TTL, one unchunked `unpack`, and a beat
    time read from the client clock;
  - both backends: `since` reset on every beat;
  - the contract: `rank` by Python's `hash()`, and `bool` accepted as a report value and
    as a load;
  - the recipe: its first version (beat, wait one `ttl`, beat);
  - each of the six review fixes below, reverted one at a time.
- **Patch coverage** over the fleet's tests, unit and Redis: 294 of 296 added lines. The two
  uncovered lines were the integer digit-limit refusal, which now has a test.

**Findings.**

1. LOW, fixed: `decode_report` accepted any JSON object, so a foreign entry carrying a list,
   a NaN or a `bool` entered the view. It now refuses what `encode_report` would not write,
   and the line is skipped.
2. LOW, fixed: the skipped-entry warning logged the whole line, report included. It now
   names the member id only.
3. LOW, fixed: the changelog said the policies agree "across replicas"; they agree for
   replicas holding the same view.
4. MEDIUM, fixed in the docs (review): a rolling deploy leaves tenants unowned under
   startup assignment. Logged above; the recipe now states the all-at-once requirement.
5. LOW, fixed (review): the mock read its clock outside its lock, so a view could hold an
   entry newer than its own `at`. The clock is now read under the lock. **Untested**: the
   race needs two threads interleaving at one point, and no test pins it.
6. LOW, fixed (review): a lone surrogate in an id or a report raised `UnicodeEncodeError`
   instead of a `fleet.*` refusal.
7. LOW, fixed (review): one integer load past the float range made every replica's
   `excess` raise `OverflowError`. Such a load is now not a number.
8. LOW, fixed (review): `assign_shard` waited by its own spec's `ttl` but let the entries
   live by the port's. It now beats with the `ttl` it waits by.
9. LOW, fixed (review): a forked child kept its ancestors' ids, and `excess` could return
   a negative count when `smallest` was below 1.
10. LOW, accepted: `Member` and `FleetView` cannot be hashed, because the report is a
    read-only mapping. Five other value objects in the tree share this, and nothing hashes
    them.
11. LOW, accepted: `share` of a negative total follows `divmod` rather than refusing.

**Residue.**

- The step is pending.
- Startup assignment is still a snapshot; rolling deploys are documented, not solved.
- No cluster client exists, so the hash tag is untested against a cluster.
- `just conformance` in its executed mode, across every engine, was not run; the fleet's
  own legs were.
