## Self-audit — 2026-09-19

Whole branch, 4 commits / ~3.5k lines (20 source, 12 test, 4 docs files), against
`6253fbb69`. Two findings, both fixed; both were composition defects, and neither was
reachable from a test that drove the temporal arm alone.

**1 — the dated reads answered with rows the aggregate hides (fixed, `057e9e3a1`).** Soft
deletion and versioning install their exclusions on the *mapper* slots every generated read
shares. `effective_on` and `timeline` build their own filter and passed through no mapper, so a
soft-deleted contract was "in force" while the aggregate's own list, get and search all hid it.
The kit hands its restrictions to the arm now. Found by pass 2 — the wrapper against every state
of what it wraps — and reproduced before the fix.

**2 — the bitemporal composition could not work (fixed, `b34b6579f`).** §4 says declaring both
arms is supported. Writing the leg that sentence implies showed every correction refused: the
successor carries its predecessor's dates, so two rows under one key hold the same period. The
combination is refused at build with a message naming what is missing, and the missing thing —
a non-overlap guarantee restricted to current versions — is proposed back to RFC 0066 rather
than worked around here.

**Checks that ran.** Full suite under the integration profile: **16132 passed, 13 skipped**.
`just quality` 19/19. **Patch coverage 100 % (326/326 added lines)**, measured with Postgres and
Mongo in the profile. Sabotage sweep of eleven load-bearing checks — the mock's overlap
comparison, each bound of both filters, the restriction plumbing, the empty-period refusal, the
required guarantee, the model/policy bounds agreement, the exact key match, and Postgres's
bounds and key comparisons — **11/11 killed**.

**What the sabotage sweep did not find, and coverage did.** The sweep came back clean and the
coverage pass then named ten uncovered lines, six of them branches nothing had exercised: the
kit's own `facade()` on a dated aggregate, `validity_facade` and its refusal, the empty-key
refusal, a two-argument `daterange` reading as the default convention, and the detection branch
for a constraint carrying the right key over the wrong dates. A clean sweep measures the
auditor's imagination; the branch nobody thought to mutate is the one coverage is for.

**Residue.**

- The concurrent-insert case §6 lists is not tested as a race, and the leg that stands in its
  place says so in its own docstring. Two writers on one pooled client serialise, and a leg
  asserting "exactly one survived" would pass against a sequential store.
- Startup compares the exclusion constraint's key columns, its period columns and its bounds —
  not that its range expression is over *those* columns in that order. A constraint over
  `daterange(valid_to, valid_from, '[]')` would pass the check. The same asymmetry the
  uniqueness pass documents: comparing what an expression *means* is the database's job.
- `_range_function` picks the range constructor for the printed DDL from the start column's
  type. Only `date` is reachable through the kit today; the timestamp arms are reachable through
  a hand-written guarantee and are pinned by their own unit leg rather than against a server.
- §7 asks for the sentence "``valid_to`` is inclusive at a date grain and exclusive at an
  instant grain". The docs do not say that, because decision 3 settles it the other way: the
  aggregate declares its convention rather than inheriting one from its grain.
