# T-0071 — Lifetime leadership, phases 1 and 2

Executing phases 1 and 2 of [RFC 0071](../rfcs/0071-lifetime-leadership.md) on
`feat/lifetime-leadership`: a lifecycle step that elects one replica to run a body for as long
as it holds a renewing lease, with per-term owners, two deadlines measured from the send time,
release after the body, the late-acquire rule, the `claim` hook, `term.token`, `may_campaign`,
`verify`, the metrics, and handoff on request through `{key}:want`. Phase 3 (`fence_floor`)
waits for the fence-advance capability (RFC 0072), which is not on this base; until then the
step refuses a `fence_floor` (entry 2).

The author is away, so every gap below is decided here and carries a proposed row.

## Self-audit — 2026-10-15

**Scope.** The whole branch against `main`: 13 commits; about 1,300 source lines (the step is
1,100 of them, half docstrings), 1,300 test lines, 430 lines of docs and this log. RFC 0071
§5–§7 and the decision table were re-read before the diff.

**Checks run.**

- `just test tests/unit` — 17,529 passed, 13 skipped (before the audit's added tests; the
  leadership file was re-run alone after them: 43 passed).
- Integration, every file that exercises a lock: `test_redis_leadership.py`,
  `test_redis_dlock.py`, the Postgres and Mongo HLC checkpoint and serialization suites, the
  authn revocation suite — 72 passed. The Redis leadership file ran four times, green each.
- Sabotage, by hand, 22 mutants over the step: 21 killed (one by a hang — a stop the term
  never reacts to spins the supervisor, which a test then never finishes); one survived (the
  `asyncio.shield` around a straggling acquire). Nothing cancels the task that awaits a
  straggler — `_retire` waits with `asyncio.wait`, and loop shutdown cancels both tasks
  anyway — so the mutant is equivalent, and the shield is removed. Two more mutants against
  the Redis file (shared owner string, a grace on `lost`) were killed there too.
- Patch coverage with the unit, example and Redis tests: 94% of the added lines. The uncovered
  lines were read; the failure branches among them (a kill or stop while an acquire is in
  flight, a release that raises, a campaign hook's configuration error, the want-lock moving to
  a fresh string) gained tests, each then sabotaged and killed.

**Findings**, each fixed on the branch:

1. **MEDIUM — fencing refused wrapped ports** (entry above; its own commit with a red-first
   test). Any port wrapper made a fencing backend fail a `requires_fencing_token` spec.
2. **MEDIUM — an asker that cannot acquire churned the unit**: holders yielded to it at
   every renewal. One-ttl cool-off after giving up (entry above).
3. **MEDIUM — the three-miss rule delayed handoff past the holder's next renewal** (D-5
   departure above).
4. **LOW — the term log lines showed asterisks**: the scrubber masks any key named like a
   token. Logged as `fence`.
5. **LOW — prose over-claimed**: the step's docstring and the fleets page said no successor
   overlaps a body that ignores cancellation; one can, after the ttl, which is fencing's job.
6. **LOW — the metrics callback iterated a dict the loop may grow** from a reader thread; it
   copies it now.
7. **LOW — untested rules**: renewals through a stopped grace, deferral to an asker, and the
   failure branches in the coverage item above had no test that failed without them.

**What I still distrust.**

- The Redis tests run on real time with a 1.5 s lease and half a second of slack. Stable here;
  a loaded CI runner could flake them.
- The stop budget is `yield_grace + release_timeout + 1 s`, as the RFC sets it. Releasing the
  want-lock and waiting for a straggler at stop each take up to `release_timeout` more; on a
  slow backend the loop can be cancelled first, and the want-lock then lapses at its ttl.
- A lease key that ends in `:want` collides with another key's want-lock. Not guarded.
- A term cancelled from outside (a kill, a stop that overran) neither sets `term.ended` nor
  counts in `terms_ended`; its body is cancelled outright.
- With handoff on, a replica that defers reads the want-lock (and the lease, while the
  want-lock is held) on every campaign tick. The RFC's "one owner read per renewal" is the
  holder's cost; the deferring replicas' is not stated there.

**Phase 3** (`fence_floor`) is not built: the step refuses it until the fence-advance
capability lands on this branch's base.
