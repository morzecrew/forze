## Self-audit findings — 2026-09-09

Adversarial pass over the whole branch after execution: 2 commits at the time of the audit,
6 files, +1527 lines (`src/forze_mock/persistence.py` new at 562 lines, one 802-line test
file, docs, changelog and this log). Ranked by severity; all fixed on the branch.

**1 · A capture ran on a worker thread, where two of the mock's own writers race it.**
`save` was called through `asyncio.to_thread`, so the deep copy of every persisted store ran
off the event loop. Holding `state.lock` does not make that safe: `MvccTx.commit` publishes
its overlay into `state.documents` with `dict.__setitem__`, and a journal rollback replays
its undo thunks the same way, and **neither takes the lock** — both are correct on the loop,
where nothing can interleave them. Reproduced as `RuntimeError: dictionary changed size
during iteration` from a `deepcopy` holding the lock against an unlocked writer. Fixed by
splitting `save` into `capture` (on the loop, under the lock) and `write` (the half that is
private to its caller and may leave). Logged above as an unlisted decision.

**2 · `release` could replace the outcome it trailed.** It is called from the shutdown
hook's `finally` and the startup hook's failure branch. An `OSError` from `flock`/`close`
would have propagated in place of the write error an operator needs, or the refusal that
explains why startup stopped. It now logs and returns, and closes the descriptor — which is
what releases the lock, so the separate unlock was removed rather than made quiet.

**3 · A header-valid file with a foreign payload raised `KeyError`.** The fingerprint pins
the field *set*, not that the payload is a mapping at all. The first missing key surfaced as
a bare `KeyError` out of a lifecycle hook, saying nothing about the file that caused it.
`read` now refuses it like any other unreadable snapshot.

**4 · Dead exception clause.** The flush loop guarded `except asyncio.CancelledError: raise`
in front of `except Exception:`. `CancelledError` is a `BaseException`, so the clause cannot
execute. Removed, with a comment saying why the bare `except Exception` is already correct.
The same clause in `forze/application/execution/background/periodic.py:99` is equally dead —
**not fixed, outside this branch's scope**, and reported here rather than swept in.

**5 · An over-claim in prose.** The dropped `authn_events` was said to leave the file with
no class coupling at all. It does not: a document namespace becomes a `JournalingStore` on
its first write, and whatever an application puts in a mock store is pickled with it —
confirmed by reading the pickle stream, which references
`forze_mock.adapters._journal`. What dropping the field actually buys is that forze's own
*contract* classes stay out of a disposable format. Corrected in the docstring and the
changelog.

**6 · Stale prose from fix 1.** The class docstring still described the hooks as calling
`acquire`/`load`/`save`/`release`. Rewritten to the six methods they now call, and to why
each pair is split.

### What the audit ran

- `just quality` — 18 gates, green, before and after.
- `pytest tests/unit` — 13,349 passed, 7 skipped.
- `pytest tests/unit/test_forze_dst` — 940 passed (40 selected by `-k "crash or restart"`).
- **Sabotage: 25 mutations, 24 killed.** Four of the first round were badly aimed (deleting a
  field name is killed by the exhaustiveness check at import, which is a different guard than
  the one under test) and one was not a mutation at all (`pass` before a `raise`); all five
  were re-aimed and killed. The single survivor is dropping `os.fsync` from the write, which
  no deterministic test can kill — it only matters across a power cut. Two further mutations
  that survived the first attempt (a `capture` and an `install` that take no lock) were killed
  after adding the lock-acquisition tests: the race itself cannot be scheduled deterministically,
  so the acquisition is asserted structurally and the reason is written next to it.
- **Patch coverage: 100%** of `src/forze_mock/persistence.py`, statements and branches, under
  the mock unit profile. Two gaps found and closed: the flush loop's failure branch (a failed
  flush must not end the loop) and `loop_name`, which was never read because nothing drove the
  stop through `ctx.drainables` — the test now does, which is what proves the registration is
  real rather than decorative.

### Residue — what would still be distrusted

- **The single-writer lock is `flock`, and `flock` is advisory and POSIX-local.** Two
  processes on the same NFS mount, or a filesystem without working `flock`, get no refusal.
  Out of scope by §4's "one process" non-goal, but it is a refusal that can silently not
  happen.
- **`storage_multipart` persists**, so an in-progress multipart upload survives a restart.
  Deliberate — S3 keeps multipart sessions across a server restart — but it is the one PERSIST
  member whose disposition was argued from the production analogue rather than measured.
- **A value-shape refactor is not caught by the fingerprint.** It digests field *names*. A
  changed value class is caught only if it fails to unpickle; one that unpickles into a stale
  object restores silently. §9 names the risk; nothing here narrows it.
- **The classification's import-time check runs on every `import forze_mock`.** That is the
  design (D-3), and it means a future field added to `MockState` breaks the import of the whole
  mock package until it is classified. Loud by intent, and worth knowing before the first
  person meets it in an unrelated test run.

Second adversarial pass, over the fixes the first one produced (`8e5a70c22`, `1d01c694a`,
`93afdf2df`) and a fresh read of the module rather than only of that diff. Three findings, all
fixed.

**1 · A non-positive `flush_every` spins the flush loop.** `BackgroundLoopControl.sleep_or_stop(0)`
returns immediately, so a zero or negative interval is not "flush often" — it is a live-lock
rewriting the whole snapshot as fast as the disk allows, while looking from the outside like the
feature working. Measured before fixing: **2,051 whole-snapshot writes in 0.25 s**. The framework's
own `periodic_lifecycle_step` refuses exactly this at wiring and says why
(`src/forze/application/execution/background/periodic.py:145-147`); this reuses that loop
primitive and had not carried its guard across. Refused in `__attrs_post_init__` now.

**2 · A path naming no file builds and fails later, obscurely.** `Path("")`, `Path(".")` and
`Path("/")` all constructed, then raised `ValueError: PosixPath('.') has an empty name` out of a
startup hook — from `lock_path`, which needs a filename to be a sibling of. Refused at
construction, naming the setting.

**3 · An unopenable snapshot path surfaced as a bare errno.** Only `FileNotFoundError` was
special-cased, so a directory at the configured path — or a permission the process lacks — left
`IsADirectoryError` escaping a lifecycle hook without naming which configured path produced it.
Wrapped like every other unreadable snapshot.

The three round-1 fixes themselves re-audited clean: the `capture`/`write` split has no caller
left passing a live state across a thread boundary, `release` is quiet on every path, and the
payload-shape check is reached before any key is read. The one thing changed there was a name —
`install` and `_install` differed by an underscore and did different things, so the private one is

### Residue, unchanged from round 1

persists on the strength of the production analogue rather than a measurement; the fingerprint

## Review round 1 — PR #416, 2026-09-09

Three findings, all from CodeAnt; CodeRabbit approved with no actionable comments and Codecov
reported every modified line covered. Codacy's summary is green and posted no inline finding —
its three "critical security" counts are the two pickle rules (`B403`, `B301`) already carrying
`nosec` locally, which is the same finding as the third below.

**1 · A field's value is not checked against the shape that field holds** — `Major`, fixed in
`7b853acb8`. Reproduced first: with the header intact and `documents` set to `42`, the value
installed and the state came back holding an int where a store belongs. This lands exactly on
the residue round 1 of the self-audit had already written down — the fingerprint digests field
*names*, so a build where a store's type changed but its name did not still matches. Values are
now checked against the declared default's type (`isinstance`, so a `JournalingStore` still
passes) before any of them is written.

**2 · `save` can be called from a worker thread, where `capture` races the MVCC commit** —
`Major`, answered in `518aadeca`. Half right: the race is real and it is why `save` is two
methods, and the wired path already calls `capture` on the loop and `write` in a thread. What
was missing is that `save` itself — the method a caller reaches for first — said nothing about
the constraint. Documented rather than re-engineered.

**3 · `pickle.loads` runs before payload validation, so whoever can replace the file can run
code at startup** — `Critical`, partly refuted and partly fixed in `58feb34f5`. The stated cause
is wrong and the difference decides the fix: a payload cannot be validated before it is
unpickled, and there is no allowlist of classes to unpickle *into*, because the mock's stores
hold whatever an application put in them. Reordering the checks buys nothing. What can be
checked is the *premise* that the file is self-written: a snapshot this process wrote is a
regular file, mode 0600, owned by this user, and one that is group- or world-writable or owned
by another uid is now refused rather than restored — judged on the descriptor about to be read,
so there is no window between the check and the read. Reproduced before (a `chmod 666` snapshot
loaded silently) and after.

**Found in the same pass, not by a reviewer:** the top-level `import fcntl` made
`import forze_mock` fail on any platform without it, where before this branch the package
imported everywhere. Moved into `acquire`, which now refuses with a message saying the rest of
the mock is unaffected (`518aadeca`).

## Review round 2 — PR #416, 2026-09-09

Three findings, all from cubic; CodeRabbit and CodeAnt added nothing new.

**1 · A stale periodic flush can roll the final snapshot back** — `P1`, fixed in `df7a3af88`.
The one genuine defect this review found, and it was mine, not a reviewer's misreading:
stopping the flush loop cancels the coroutine awaiting a write, never the worker thread
performing one, so a flush that outlives the five-second stop grace keeps running while
shutdown starts its own. Each `os.replace` is atomic on its own, so whichever lands last wins,
and the stale one carries an older capture. Reproduced by forcing that interleaving —
`landed:final` then `landed:periodic`, and the snapshot came back without the state shutdown
had just captured. Writes are serialized now and shutdown's is marked final; a write still in
flight when the final one has landed is dropped. Both halves are load-bearing, which the
sabotage sweep showed: with only the lock, removing the mark survived, because the lock orders
writes by *arrival* and a thread already dispatched can arrive after the final one.

**2 · A field in two buckets is accepted at import** — `P2`, fixed in `59024a185`. The union
adds up, so neither existing check fires, and a restore then applies the persist pass and the
reset pass in the same call — the value comes back and is thrown away. The battery already
asserted pairwise disjointness; the import did not.

**3 · The changelog entry is far past the house cap** — `P3`, fixed in `b7021fc99`. True of
the rule (1,712 characters over nine sentences against 320 and three) and false in its
framing: this was not the longest entry in the file. `[Unreleased]` opens with an 1,881-char
entry, and 76 of the file's 751 entries exceed the cap, which `just quality` does not check.
Trimmed the entry this branch owns; the repo-wide drift is a separate sweep and was said so on
the thread rather than silently swept in here.

`just quality` 18/18 · `pytest tests/unit` 13,361 passed · coverage of
`src/forze_mock/persistence.py` still 100% · every thread answered and resolved,
`account` exits 0.

## Review round 3 — PR #416, 2026-09-09

One finding, from cubic, and it caught a vacuous pass this branch had introduced.

**A `write` test double dropped the new keyword** — `P2`, fixed in `4b5cc1b9b`. Adding
`final=True` to the shutdown hook's write broke the double in
`test_a_failed_flush_does_not_end_the_loop`, which took the payload positionally and nothing
else. The lifecycle runner logs and swallows whatever a shutdown hook raises
(`run.py:295-303`), so the resulting `TypeError` never surfaced and the test stayed green over
a shutdown that had stopped happening.

The double now carries the whole signature, and — the half that matters — the test asserts the
shutdown write landed, so a double that drops a keyword fails there rather than quietly
elsewhere. Verified by restoring the old double: the test goes red. It was the only wrong
`write` double in the suite; the other patches `_land`, below the keyword.

**The rule this earns:** widening a method's signature can silently disable a test whose
double is narrower, and a swallowing caller turns that into a pass. When a signature grows, the
doubles of it are part of the change — and a test whose teardown does real work should assert
that the work happened.

`just quality` 18/18 · coverage of `src/forze_mock/persistence.py` still 100% · every thread
answered and resolved, `account` exits 0.

**Drift count: 0.**
