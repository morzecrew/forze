## Self-audit — 2026-09-10

Scope: the whole branch against `main` — 5 commits, 15 source files (+1846/-122), 8 test
files, 7 docs, 3 config, 1 CI. Ten passes walked in order, sabotage before coverage.

**Findings, most severe first. All fixed on the branch.**

1. **A program the image does not have came back as `exited` 127** where the process tier
   answers `spawn_failed`. The init process always starts, so the run "succeeded" into a
   non-zero exit — one plane giving two accounts of one mistake, decided by wiring the
   caller cannot see, which is exactly what row 24's shim marker exists to prevent below.
   Fixed by reading status and the init's own marker together. Verified red first.
2. **`CapDrop: ["ALL"]` was unproven, and its test said otherwise.** The test read `CapEff`,
   which a non-root uid empties on its own, so it passed with every capability still
   available to be regained. Found by sabotage: removing the drop changed nothing. It reads
   `CapBnd` now — `0` against `a80425fb` without the drop.
3. **`HostConfig.Init` was proven by nothing and its recorded reason was wrong.** See the
   09:15Z entry: a CPU over-run is nameable without it, and what it actually buys is the
   graceful half of every kill. Pinned by elapsed time — 7.09s against a 5s bound with the
   flag off.
4. **A route naming `/` as its workspace staged into the container root and collected
   nothing**, silently. Refused at config time now.
5. **The reader's cancellation could be mistaken for the caller's.** A `suppress` around
   `await reader` swallows both, and the second belongs to somebody who cancelled; the run
   would then finish and hand a result back. Replaced with `asyncio.wait`, which never
   re-raises the awaited task's own outcome. Justified by construction — the window is one
   loop turn wide and I could not make it deterministic.
6. **The workspace directory entry was unproven.** Every test staged a program, and adding
   a file already creates its parents, so the entry only matters for a request that stages
   nothing — which had no test. Without it that run exits 1 on permissions.
7. **Two branches were dead** and were removed rather than kept: `put_archive`'s
   client-error mapping (the daemon answers 404 for every shape I could produce, and the
   adapter always extracts at `/`, which exists) and `download`'s 404 tolerance (the child
   cannot remove its own workspace, and the port's contract says a collection failure
   raises).
8. **The frame reassembly was untestable and untested.** A frame split across two reads is
   decided by the daemon's write boundaries, and no test could ask for one — a 300KB single
   write did not produce one. Extracted as `demultiplex`, which is handed the split
   directly; eleven cases now cover every offset through a header and into a payload.

**Verification.** Sabotage: ten mutations against the load-bearing guards — the glob
filter, `CapDrop`, `NetworkMode`, `Init`, the workspace entry, the escape guard, the
removal, secret masking, the exec marker and the run identity. Three survived the first
sweep and each was finding 2, 3 or 6; all ten are killed now. Patch coverage over the added
lines, measured with the unit suites and the container legs together: **100% (613/613)**.
Full suite green — 13765 passed, 7 skipped — and all 18 `just quality` gates pass.

**Residue.** The run's wall-clock budget bounds the log follow and nothing after it: a
wedged daemon would hang `wait`, `inspect` or the archive read past the ceiling that exists
to prevent exactly that. Left as it is deliberately — every call in the adapter is equally
exposed, `POST /wait` is inherently long-blocking so it cannot carry a read timeout, and a
guard on one call would be theatre with an untestable branch behind it. It belongs in the
docs' limits, not in a defensive branch nobody can exercise.

One further thing I could not reproduce: a single failure of the stdin round-trip inside a
full-file run, which passed 12 times in isolation and three more full-file runs afterwards.
Recorded rather than explained.
