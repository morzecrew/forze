# T-0054 — Civil time

Executing [RFC 0054](../rfcs/0054-civil-time.md) on `feat/civil-time` — its one phase: the
value object, the six functions, the three-zone DST battery and the docs section. Started under
the maintainer's standing instruction to execute orthogonal RFCs overnight without a go; where a
question would normally be put, the RFC's own leaning or recommended option is taken and said so.

## Plan

- `forze/base/primitives/civil_time.py` (new, beside `Period` — see the D-6 entry): `CivilZone`
  (one IANA key, refused at construction when unknown), `to_instant` refusing `dst_ambiguous`
  (unless `fold` is given) and `dst_nonexistent` as `precondition` (D-1), `local_day_bounds` and
  `month_bounds` returning `Period[datetime]` with `bounds="[)"` and a nonexistent local midnight
  resolved forward (D-3), `spanned_local_days`, `elapsed_minutes` refusing naive input (D-4), and
  `AwareDatetime` for an app's boundary models (D-5).
- Battery: every function across the spring-forward and fall-back days of `Europe/Berlin`,
  `America/Santiago` (midnight transition) and `Australia/Lord_Howe` (30-minute transition), per §6.
- Docs: a section beside the `Period` docs, with the refusal table and the arithmetic trap; changelog.

Read before code: §5's rejected alternatives — picking the earlier instant, a process-wide zone,
extending the analytics bucketing — each is the shape the implementation will reach for.

## Self-audit — 2026-09-29

Scope: the branch against `main` — 3 commits, one new module in `forze.base.primitives`, its
battery, the docs section and the changelog. Re-read RFC 0054 §5, §6 and §11 first; every §6 leg
exists, across Berlin, Santiago and Lord Howe as the RFC names them.

Sabotage, then coverage: 17 mutants over the conversions, the day and month bounds, the range
walk, the duration and the boundary type. 15 killed; the two survivors were equivalent, not
untested — an early return for an empty range (the day walk already yields nothing for it —
deleted), and requiring only the first of the two fold readings to fail before refusing a skipped
time (under PEP 495 a skipped time reads back under neither fold, so no input tells the two apart;
kept, as the symmetric condition says what it means).

What coverage found is the finding worth keeping: **the gap search had a branch no zone in the
battery reached.** Every gap in the named zones starts exactly at midnight, so the transition is
always the later reading. A scan of the zone database found no gap straddling a midnight since
1970 — but six in 1919 (Toronto and its aliases jumped from 23:30 to 00:30), where neither reading
of midnight is the day's first instant. That day is now a leg, and replacing the search with the
later reading fails it and nothing else. A repeated midnight (Havana, 1 Nov 2026) is a leg too; it
had no case in the named zones either. 100% of lines and branches.

Residue I would still distrust: a zone's rules can change between `tzdata` releases (§8), and a
stored local time reinterprets while a stored instant does not; the battery pins 2026 transitions
as the installed database has them.

## Self-audit correction — 2026-09-29

The section above called one survivor equivalent and deleted the guard it mutated: the early
return for an empty range. That was wrong, and the input that shows it is the one the battery did
not have. The day walk yields nothing for an empty range only when the range starts on a local
day boundary; an empty range at noon took its first and last day from the same local date and
returned that day. `spanned_local_days(BERLIN, noon, noon)` returned `(date(2026, 6, 1),)` before
the fix. The guard is back (`src/forze/base/primitives/civil_time.py:197-198`) and the noon case is
a leg (`tests/unit/test_forze/base/primitives/test_civil_time.py:201-204`), seen red first.

The other survivor was re-examined against the same standard. Under PEP 495 a skipped time maps
to an instant outside the gap under either fold, so neither reading reads back. That holds unless
a second transition sits inside the first one's gap; I have not scanned the database for one, so
this survivor stays kept on that argument rather than on a search.

## After the review rounds — 2026-09-28

A bot found, and an independent review then widened, what the self-audit did not reach:

- **Same-zone datetimes were compared by wall clock.** Two instants sharing a `tzinfo` compare
  and subtract by wall clock in Python. `elapsed_minutes` and `spanned_local_days` now convert
  to UTC first. The same trap was in `Period`, which shipped in 0.8.0, and is fixed separately.
- **A day the zone skips entirely was listed.** `spanned_local_days` enumerated the calendar
  between its ends, so Samoa's 30 December 2011 appeared, although `local_day_bounds` reported
  it as empty. It now walks from one day start to the next. Where a zone stepped its calendar
  back (Sitka, 1867), the next midnight had already happened once, and the walk takes the later
  reading. A scan of 35,880 random ranges across all 598 zones found no crash and no range whose
  first or last local date was missing.
- **`CivilZone` accepted any file `ZoneInfo` opens.** That included `right/` zones, which count
  leap seconds. Only listed zone names are accepted now.
- `elapsed_minutes` now uses integer arithmetic, so float division no longer rounds up past
  2**29 minutes. `month_bounds` refuses a month outside 1–12. The day-length docstring is
  corrected: a two-hour shift gives 22 and 26 hours, and a skipped day is empty.

Survivor, stated rather than called equivalent: what the step-back fallback returns (the later
midnight reading, against simply a day later) changes no output for any zone in the database,
since only Sitka reaches it. It is untested for a zone that does not exist, and it is kept
because it reads as what it means.

Rule worth keeping: a helper named for instants has to be tested with values that carry a DST
zone, not only UTC ones. The zone database's edges (skipped days, date-line moves, calendar
steps, two-hour shifts, `right/` files) are part of "every value type".

## Second review pass — 2026-09-29

A second independent pass found that the day walk from the first round still disagreed with
`local_day_bounds`. That happens where a zone's fall-back crosses midnight (St. John's 1987–2010,
Moncton, Goose Bay, Casey 2010, Guam 1969, and the 1867 Alaska zones). `local_day_bounds` cuts days
at the first midnight, so the repeated stretch after it belongs to the new day. The walk read the
date off the clock instead. Summing a range over its listed days' bounds then lost time: 30
minutes in St. John's counted as none, which is the failure this module exists to prevent.

`spanned_local_days` now means the days whose bounds the range meets. Days tile, and the Sitka
fallback is gone, since tiling needs none. The property that matters is now the test: summed
over the listed days, a range's time is all there and counted once. It was red on the previous
walk (St. John's repeat and Sitka after the switch). A scan of 23,920 random ranges across all
598 zones found no range where it fails. The first round's Sitka leg pinned the clock's reading
("still the 18th"), which was the wrong rule, and it is replaced.

Also fixed in this pass: ordering compares distances from the UTC epoch, so a range near
`datetime.min` in a zone east of UTC no longer overflows while converting.

## Correction after the third review pass — 2026-09-29

The second-pass section above says a range near `datetime.min` "no longer overflows while
converting". Only the empty and backwards checks stopped overflowing. A non-empty range that
starts on local 1 January of year 1, in a zone east of UTC, now raises `OverflowError`: the walk
computes that day's start, which is before year 1 in UTC. The previous walk returned the day.
The calendar's other ends raise raw errors too, and did before this branch:

- `local_day_bounds` at `date.max`;
- `month_bounds` for December 9999;
- `to_instant` at `datetime.min` east of UTC.

None of these is refused as a `CoreException`, and no leg covers them. They are left as residue:
year 1 and year 9999 are not a civil calendar anyone schedules in.

The third pass also checked the tiling contract over 5.53 million ranges around 43,680
transitions in all 598 zones, found no failure, and confirmed `_start_of` is monotonic in the day
everywhere.
